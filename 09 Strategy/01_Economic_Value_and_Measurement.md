## Purpose

This document states RF-One's commercial position on economic value and how it may be measured, replacing the legacy absolute claims recorded in `90 Archive/Legacy Repository/X00 Knowledge Repository/01 Objectives/Objectives.md` with the approved, bounded formulation.

---

## 1. Measurable economic value as a central commercial objective

RF-One commercial Products should aim to create measurable economic value for the business customers that use them. This is central to RF-One's commercial strategy.

It is not, and must not become, a universal Core Goal. The Core models a Subject in relation to Reality generally — a Subject's Desires and Goals need not be economic, and Core must remain capable of representing non-economic Desires without judging them as invalid or lesser (see Desire ≠ Goal, [../00 Core/ConceptualArchitecture/02_Desire_Goal_and_Reality_Check.md](../00%20Core/ConceptualArchitecture/02_Desire_Goal_and_Reality_Check.md)). "RF-One commercial Products pursue economic value" is a fact about RF-One's business, not a fact about what every Subject must want.

Pursuit of economic value is always subject to:

- confirmed customer Goals;
- Constraints;
- Delegated Authority;
- applicable law/policy;
- acceptable risk;
- available Domain knowledge;
- sufficient Reality information.

RF-One must never override a customer's confirmed Goals in pursuit of profit. Economic value is something RF-One helps a customer achieve toward goals the customer has confirmed, not a license to substitute RF-One's own priorities for the customer's.

---

## 2. Value metrics vary by Product, Domain and customer Goal

RF-One does not mandate one universal economic metric. The correct measure of value depends on the Product, the Domain, and the specific Goals a given customer has confirmed. Applicable measures may include, without this being an exhaustive or mandatory list:

- margin;
- contribution;
- cash flow;
- EBITDA;
- revenue;
- avoided cost;
- labor productivity;
- inventory efficiency;
- risk reduction;
- capital efficiency;
- customer lifetime value;
- other Domain/Product-specific measures.

This document does not define a detailed finance ontology. Choosing and formalizing specific metrics for a given Product or Domain is future Product/Domain work.

---

## 3. Cash-Based Profit as a historical metric candidate

The legacy definition of profit — cash received minus cash paid, treated as the platform's sole primary optimization target — is **not** preserved as the universal or permanent RF-One economic metric.

Cash-Based Profit remains a strategically useful **historical metric candidate / possible Product metric** that may be used where appropriate for a given Product or customer context, alongside the other measures listed in Section 2. It is one option among several, not a mandatory default.

---

## 4. Counterfactual value measurement

RF-One should aim to estimate the value it generated by comparing:

```text
actual observed outcome
```

against:

```text
estimated counterfactual outcome without RF-One intervention
```

where such estimation is epistemically defensible. This is strategically important for demonstrating B2B return on investment: a customer needs to understand not just what happened, but what RF-One's involvement plausibly changed.

### Required safeguards

- An estimated counterfactual must never be presented as a Fact. It is, at best, a well-supported Inference or Hypothesis — see the Epistemic Boundary, [../00 Core/ConceptualArchitecture/05_Epistemic_Boundary_and_Subject_Sovereignty.md](../00%20Core/ConceptualArchitecture/05_Epistemic_Boundary_and_Subject_Sovereignty.md).
- Any value attribution should make its uncertainty, assumptions, and supporting evidence visible rather than presenting a single confident number.
- This does not redefine the generic Core concept of Outcome ([../00 Core/ConceptualArchitecture/03_Decision_Action_Outcome_Learning.md](../00%20Core/ConceptualArchitecture/03_Decision_Action_Outcome_Learning.md)) around financial counterfactuals. Outcome remains "what actually happens as a result of an Action, as observed in Reality" for any Domain; counterfactual value estimation is a commercial/Product-level use of that Outcome data specific to B2B ROI demonstration, not a change to what Outcome means in Core.

## 5. Operational vs. strategic value horizons

RF-One should reason across multiple economic time horizons, recognizing at minimum:

- **Operational horizon** — value expected to materialize soon after a Decision is acted on; operational recommendations and actions should aim to generate measurable benefit as early as reasonably possible.
- **Strategic horizon** — longer-term initiatives whose acceptable return period depends on the specific strategy and must be evaluated on its own terms rather than judged by operational-horizon expectations.

This is a minimum distinction, not a rigid two-bucket model; a Product or Domain may introduce more granular horizons where useful.

**Short-term optimization must not silently destroy long-term value.** A Decision that improves an operational-horizon metric while damaging a strategic-horizon Goal, Constraint, or relationship (for example customer trust, quality, or compliance posture) is not a valid optimization merely because it improves the metric being watched most closely. See Optimization Boundaries, [../00 Core/Process.md](../00%20Core/Process.md), and Temporal Coherence, [../00 Core/ConceptualArchitecture/04_Temporal_Coherence_and_Evolution.md](../00%20Core/ConceptualArchitecture/04_Temporal_Coherence_and_Evolution.md), for the Core-level reasoning capabilities this depends on.
