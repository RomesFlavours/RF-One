## Purpose

This document states RF-One's positioning as a Business Knowledge Platform rather than commodity software, and the current strategic preference for centralized/service delivery — replacing the legacy absolute claim that "RF-ONE can never be sold as software" with a reviewable strategic position.

---

## 1. Business Knowledge Platform positioning

RF-One's proprietary value lies primarily in the accumulated and structured combination of:

- Core ontology;
- Domain knowledge;
- Subject/Reality modeling;
- Decision and Outcome memory;
- temporal coherence;
- epistemic discipline;
- orchestration;
- autonomy logic;
- Process knowledge;
- constraints;
- learning;
- governance.

Software is essential execution machinery — without it, none of the above can observe, decide, act, or learn in Reality. But the strategic asset RF-One is building is broader than the software code that implements it at any given moment. **This does not mean software has no value**; it means software is not, by itself, the defensible advantage.

---

## 2. Intelligence Engines are components, not RF-One itself

RF-One remains architecturally distinct from the AI models it uses to reason (Intelligence Engines — see [../00 Core/ConceptualArchitecture/06_Business_Autopilot_and_Intelligence_Engine.md](../00%20Core/ConceptualArchitecture/06_Business_Autopilot_and_Intelligence_Engine.md)). Strategically, this matters because a competitive advantage built on a specific third-party model is not defensible — any competitor can buy the same model. RF-One's defensibility must come from what is built around and on top of Intelligence Engines, not from the engines themselves.

Commodity intelligence engines, models, infrastructure, OCR, APIs, databases, and other technologies may be bought rather than rebuilt when appropriate, consistent with `../CLAUDE.md` ("External Technology"): RF-One should not reinvent commodity capability, and should keep its proprietary value in model, logic, knowledge, orchestration, decision processes, and learning mechanisms.

---

## 3. Why centralized/service delivery may compound knowledge advantage

Centralized, service-based delivery may strongly support:

- **knowledge governance** — a single, governed place where validated Business Knowledge lives, rather than divergent per-customer copies;
- **continuous improvement** — improvements benefit the platform as a whole rather than one deployment;
- **shared platform intelligence** — see [03_Shared_Intelligence_and_Knowledge_Governance.md](03_Shared_Intelligence_and_Knowledge_Governance.md) for the safeguards that govern this;
- **operational control** — RF-One can maintain the operating discipline (epistemic boundaries, authority boundaries, temporal coherence) that the Business Autopilot model depends on;
- **commercial defensibility** — a compounding, centrally governed knowledge asset is harder to replicate than a static software product.

If each customer instead evolved an independent, disconnected copy of RF-One's Core/Domain knowledge, different customers would develop different, unreconciled business ontologies, learning would stay siloed per deployment, and the accumulated Business Knowledge that is RF-One's actual asset would fragment rather than compound.

---

## 4. SaaS/service as current strategic preference, not immutable law

**SaaS/service delivery is RF-One's current strategic preference**, chosen because it currently best protects and compounds RF-One's knowledge advantage as described above.

This is explicitly **not** canonicalized as an immutable architectural law. The legacy claim that RF-One "must be delivered as a service and can never be sold as software" is not carried forward in that absolute form. Future delivery models — including on-premise, licensed, hybrid, or other arrangements not yet identified — remain legitimate future Product Owner decisions, to be evaluated against whether they still protect the knowledge advantage described in Section 3, not ruled out by default.

---

## 5. Distinction from Runtime implementation

This document states a **commercial delivery strategy**, not a Runtime architecture. It does not specify:

- cloud provider;
- tenancy architecture;
- APIs or database technology;
- deployment topology;
- specific infrastructure choices.

Those are `03 Software/` Runtime decisions, to be made consistent with this strategic direction but not defined by it. Where a Runtime implementation choice diverges from the preference stated here, that divergence should be visible at the Software/Product level (see Layer separation, [00_RF-One_Strategy.md](00_RF-One_Strategy.md)) rather than silently treated as the new strategy.
