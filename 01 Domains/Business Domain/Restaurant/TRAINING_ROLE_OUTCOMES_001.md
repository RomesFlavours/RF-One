# Restaurant Training Outcomes by Role

**Version:** 0.1 — 2026-10-27
**Status:** Working draft. The Product Owner requested documentation of what Training should achieve for each role, including employees already hired without passing through Selection. The role outcomes below are proposals for review, not approved operating standards.
**Scope:** Restaurant-specific content consumed by the Training service. Documentation only.

## 1. Purpose

Training should help each person acquire the knowledge and practical capability required to perform their assigned role in the restaurant's actual context. Start from what the person must be able to do, then identify the knowledge, pills and verification needed to reach that outcome.

The same role expectations apply whether the person arrives from Selection or is already employed. Selection evidence may supply a starting assessment; it is not a prerequisite for entering Training. Existing employees need an initial picture based on relevant evidence and any targeted assessment still required.

Completing a pill, passing a knowledge quiz and demonstrating practical capability are distinct results. Reading content alone does not establish operational readiness.

## 2. Proposed common outcomes

**Product Owner direction, 2026-10-27:** formation comes before operational training. First establish where people work, the restaurant's philosophy and legacy, the intended guest experience and each role's contribution to it. This shared foundation precedes the role-specific operational outcomes below. The [Server course working draft](SERVER_COURSE_OUTCOMES_001.md#1-formation-before-operational-training) maps the existing course and the Legacy documents supplied by the Product Owner to formation outcomes. Philosophy and legacy are existing source material to extract and teach, not content to request again or infer from generic restaurant practice.

Every role needs the parts of the following knowledge that apply to its work:

- Understand the restaurant's identity, intended guest experience and the contribution expected from the role.
- Know the approved procedures, tools and handoffs needed for the assigned tasks.
- Recognize uncertainty, mistakes and situations requiring help, and contact the appropriate person.
- Apply the restaurant's approved safety, hygiene and emergency procedures within the role's responsibilities.
- Communicate relevant information accurately to colleagues and guests, without inventing answers.

Shared knowledge may use the same underlying pills across roles. Applicability and expected capability depend on the role and context.

## 3. Proposed outcomes for each role

This is an initial discussion set, not the restaurant's confirmed role registry. Role names and responsibilities must be reconciled with the actual approved Restaurant Roles and assignments. A title alone does not grant authority or determine every task a person performs.

| Role | Result Training should achieve | Proposed evidence of readiness |
|---|---|---|
| Server | Manage the assigned guest service sequence; explain the current menu accurately; establish preferences and communicate dietary concerns through the approved process; enter and coordinate orders; recommend appropriate dishes and pairings; handle billing and escalate exceptions within assigned authority. | Explain representative dishes; handle a guest scenario with uncertainty or dietary concerns; demonstrate order entry and handoffs; complete an observed service sequence against approved criteria. |
| Host | Welcome guests, manage reservations and the waiting list using approved procedures, communicate realistic expectations and coordinate seating with the floor team. | Demonstrate arrival, booking and waiting-list scenarios; handle a seating conflict; communicate a change in table availability to the team. |
| Bartender | Prepare and present the approved beverage offering consistently, explain relevant products and pairings, organize the station and coordinate beverage service; recognize situations requiring the approved alcohol-service escalation process. | Demonstrate selected preparations against approved recipes, station setup and order coordination; respond to a service exception within assigned authority. |
| Runner or food expediter | Identify dishes and destinations, check the information required at the service handoff, coordinate delivery and flag discrepancies before proceeding. | Recognize representative dishes; demonstrate the kitchen-to-table handoff and respond to a missing or inconsistent order detail. |
| Busser or server assistant | Prepare, maintain and reset service areas to the approved standard while coordinating clearing and support with the service team. | Demonstrate setup, clearing and reset; recognize when clearing would interrupt service or remove something still needed; perform the agreed handoff. |
| Cook | Prepare assigned dishes to approved recipes, portions, presentation and workflow; apply relevant handling procedures; coordinate timing and communicate shortages, uncertainty or deviations. | Demonstrate representative preparations and station setup; follow an approved handling procedure; recognize and escalate a preparation or timing problem. |
| Dishwasher or steward | Maintain the required flow of clean equipment and utensils, use assigned equipment and products according to approved procedures, and report faults or unsafe conditions. | Demonstrate the cleaning workflow, separation and storage steps; identify an equipment or process exception and explain the required response. |
| Shift leader or FOH team leader | Coordinate the assigned shift or service area, conduct briefings and handoffs, recognize service bottlenecks, support colleagues and escalate or resolve issues within delegated authority. | Lead a simulated briefing; coordinate a disrupted service scenario; explain the boundary between an action they may take and a decision requiring approval. |
| Restaurant manager | Translate approved restaurant objectives into operating priorities, coordinate functions, monitor service and operational results, provide evidence-based coaching and govern follow-up within assigned authority. | Review an operating scenario, identify the problem and supporting evidence, propose action and follow-up, and distinguish training needs from process, staffing or tool problems. |

Kitchen leadership and other specialized roles require their own outcomes if present in the actual organization. They are not silently included in Cook or Restaurant manager. The initial set must be expanded or narrowed to match the restaurant.

## 4. From a role outcome to individual pills

For each outcome, specify:

1. The concrete task or situation and the applicable role, restaurant, location and operating context.
2. What the person must know and what they must demonstrate in practice.
3. The approved source of that knowledge and the relevant existing pills; identify missing content separately.
4. Pill-level criteria for the expected Capability level, using the existing 1–5 vocabulary, and the applicable weight and safety precedence where defined.
5. How the outcome will be verified, by whom and which evidence will be retained.

Do not assign a numeric score to a role or a grouping of pills. Do not invent universal thresholds or a conversion from the existing quiz score to the Capability scale. These require explicit criteria for each pill.

The Product Owner subsequently supplied `Course.one` as the Server course. [Server outcomes from the Rome's Flavours course](SERVER_COURSE_OUTCOMES_001.md) now provides the initial source-based refinement for that restaurant. The generic proposal below remains a starting structure, not a substitute for the supplied course. The source-based draft also covers professional presence, wine technique, service pace, teamwork, environment readiness and practical learning.

| Outcome | Knowledge to connect to pills | Verification |
|---|---|---|
| Explain a dish accurately | Approved ingredients, preparation, characteristics, available variations and the restaurant's dietary-information procedure. | Explain a current dish and respond appropriately when the guest asks something the person cannot confirm. |
| Recommend an appropriate choice | Guest preferences, relevant menu knowledge, approved pairing guidance and availability. | A guest scenario requiring a reasoned recommendation consistent with the stated preferences. |
| Coordinate a correct order | Order-entry procedure, modifiers, table/seat references where used and kitchen/bar handoffs. | Demonstration using the approved tools or an agreed simulation, including an order discrepancy. |

This decomposition starts the discussion; it is not a complete Server curriculum. Existing menu pills can supply part of the knowledge after their content and applicability are checked.

## 5. Individual entry and progress

Link the person to their existing identity and actual assignments. Compare applicable expectations with available evidence, preserving its source, date and context. Multiple roles or locations may require different expectations for the same person.

- **Not evaluated:** assessment is needed; absence of evidence is not a failing score or a known gap.
- **Not applicable:** the pill does not apply in this context and produces no training need.
- **Assessed below expectation:** prepare a targeted training path for trainer confirmation.
- **Assessed at or above expectation:** retain the evidence without assigning redundant training solely because the person did not enter through Selection.

After delivery, record what was verified and what remains to be demonstrated. Preserve earlier evidence and revisions. Changes to responsibilities, procedures or content may require a new applicability and evidence review; they do not automatically invalidate every previous result.

## 6. Ownership and decisions still needed

Organization owns work design, roles, responsibilities and assignments. Restaurant supplies its technical operating knowledge and standards. The Training service owns the training cycle; Operational Knowledge manages the pills. People retains its mandate over professional knowledge of the person. This draft does not decide new assessment ownership or visibility rules for existing employees; those must be reconciled with the current boundaries before implementation.

The next review should establish the actual roles in scope, confirm the outcomes for the first role, identify approved source material and map its outcomes to individual pills. Numeric thresholds, practical verification criteria, validators and the relationship between training verification and permission to work remain to be defined. Training completion grants no authority by itself.
