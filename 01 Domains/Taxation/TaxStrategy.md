# Tax Strategy

**Version:** 0.1
**Status:** Draft (initial canonical foundation)
**Module:** Domain / Taxation

---

## Purpose

**Tax Strategy** is a lawful approach intended to improve after-tax / retained outcomes while respecting applicable constraints.

Tax Strategy is the Taxation-Domain-specific application of Core's [Constraint Shaping](../../00%20Core/ConceptualArchitecture/08_Net_Outcome_and_Structural_Optimization.md) (Section 3): a lawful Decision/Action path that changes the Subject's future relationship to Reality — future Tax Obligations, Tax Treatments, risks, or opportunities — rather than a way of concealing or misrepresenting the Subject's current relationship to Reality.

---

## What a Tax Strategy is built from

A Tax Strategy is typically evaluated through one or more [Tax Scenarios](TaxScenario.md), comparing a current structure/timing against lawful alternatives such as, where applicable:

```text
entity selection
entity separation
ownership structure
transaction timing
compensation structure
capital expenditure timing
deduction / credit utilization
depreciation choices
loss utilization
retirement / benefit structures
jurisdictional choices
asset placement
financing choices
succession / estate structures
```

This is illustrative, not a universal checklist — see [TaxScenario.md](TaxScenario.md).

---

## Tax Strategy is not a Decision

**Tax Strategy must not replace Core Decision semantics.** A Tax Strategy is a proposed or adopted *approach*; the act of choosing to pursue it — and any Action that implements it (e.g. actually restructuring an entity, actually timing a transaction differently) — is a Core [Decision](../../00%20Core/ConceptualArchitecture/03_Decision_Action_Outcome_Learning.md) and [Action](../../00%20Core/ConceptualArchitecture/03_Decision_Action_Outcome_Learning.md) like any other, made by the Subject or under its Delegated Authority. Taxation surfaces the Strategy and its expected consequences; it does not execute it, and does not substitute itself for Subject Sovereignty — see [Taxation.md](Taxation.md), "Relationship to Decision" and "Subject and professional authority".

Where implementing a Tax Strategy requires an actual change in Accounting, Finance, or Legal Entity Management (e.g. an actual entity restructuring), that implementation belongs to those Domains/Runtimes and to the Subject's Decision — not to Taxation. See [README.md](README.md), "Taxation ≠ Core, ≠ Accounting, ≠ Finance, ≠ Legal Entity Management".

---

## Lawful boundary

A Tax Strategy must explicitly remain distinct from:

```text
evasion
fraud
misrepresentation
concealment
false reporting
sham structures
```

This reuses, and does not redefine, the lawful-optimization boundary in [../../00 Core/ConceptualArchitecture/08_Net_Outcome_and_Structural_Optimization.md](../../00%20Core/ConceptualArchitecture/08_Net_Outcome_and_Structural_Optimization.md), Section 5. The operational test: a lawful Tax Strategy genuinely changes the Subject's actual relationship to Reality — a real structure that is actually adopted, a real timing choice that is actually made, a real election that is actually filed. Evasion, fraud, misrepresentation, concealment, false reporting and sham structures instead misstate or conceal the Subject's actual relationship to Reality while pretending it is something else, or pretending a change occurred that did not.

Taxation must not treat an unlawful or deceptive alternative as an ordinary equivalent Tax Strategy to be weighed against lawful ones in a [Tax Scenario](TaxScenario.md) comparison — see [TaxScenario.md](TaxScenario.md), "Lawful alternatives only".

---

## What a Tax Strategy is not

- It is not a Decision — see above.
- It is not automatically successful — a Tax Strategy's expected [Tax Impact](TaxImpact.md) is prospective until determined; see [TaxImpact.md](TaxImpact.md), "Prospective vs. determined Tax Impact".
- It is not equivalent to nominal tax minimization at any cost — see [TaxScenario.md](TaxScenario.md), "Scenario comparison dimensions", which includes risk, compliance burden, reversibility and other tradeoffs alongside tax liability.
