All three sections match exactly for this window.

**Override semantics, validated against all 8 reference OVERRIDDEN SHIFTS rows (not just the one worked example in the task spec):** `shift.inTime`/`shift.outTime` are the raw/actual clock events; `shift.overrideInTime`/`shift.overrideOutTime`, when present, are a manager-entered correction that becomes the *official* time used in the main SHIFTS section and for Total Hours. This is the opposite of an initial reading of the field names ("override" naturally suggests "the new value," which is correct — the subtlety was that the *main* SHIFTS section uses the override, not the raw event, as the "official" clock time).

**Elapsed-hours rounding inconsistency (Clover's own export, not a reconstruction defect):** for at least one shift, the reference's SHIFTS-section "Elapsed Hours" (3.04) and OVERRIDDEN-SHIFTS-section "Overridden Elapsed Hours" (3.03) differ by 0.01h for what should be the identical in/out pair — and the OVERRIDDEN section's own "Difference" (2.77) does not exactly equal "Overridden Elapsed Hours" (3.03) minus "Actual Elapsed Hours" (0.27) = 2.76. This ~0.01h jitter appears to be a property of how Clover's own export rounds each figure independently before display, not an error in RF-One's reconstruction (which uses one consistent formula throughout).

## 5. Clover semantic inconsistencies (Tip / Service Charge) — observations only, no rule chosen

These are documented as found. **No decision is made here about which source RF-One should use for payroll tip/service-charge calculation** — that is explicitly out of scope (see the task report, §O, for the recommended next task).

1. **The same "tip" concept is represented with different missing-value semantics in Orders vs Payments.** A payment with no tip has an absent `tipAmount` field. The *Payments* export leaves that cell blank. The *Orders* export, built from the same underlying payment, shows `0.00`. Both are Clover's own dashboard behavior — confirmed on real examples, not assumed.
2. **Service Charge is structurally a Payment-adjacent concept (it is collected as part of the customer's payment) but is only exposed through Orders/Line Items, never through Payments.** No field for it exists on the Payment object at all. It is represented as a synthetic Order line item (`name: "Gratuity"`, `note: "Service Charge"`, `isOrderFee: true`) with its own `percentage` and `orderFee` reference — conceptually closer to a Discount-like order-level adjustment than to a payment attribute, yet it functions economically like a mandatory tip.
3. **Tip and Service Charge are two separate amounts that are easy to conflate.** For orders with both (e.g. Order `KX51JKX980WB0`: Tip 18.38, Service Charge 14.76), `Payments.Tip Amount` equals only the Tip portion — it does not include the Service Charge. A payroll calculation that reads only `Payments.Tip Amount` will silently miss the Service Charge entirely for such orders; a calculation that reads only `Orders.Tip` will miss the Service Charge too, since that is a separate column.
4. **This matches, and now empirically confirms, the Product Owner's own observation** that the manual Excel process sometimes has to fall back to the Order export specifically because the Payment export cannot carry Service Charge data at all (0 of 287 reference Payments rows have it populated; 29 of 271 reference Orders rows do).
5. **The line-item "Service Charge" flag (`"True"`) is the most granular source** — it identifies the exact synthetic line item and its `percentage`/`orderFee` reference, which Orders.Service Charge is simply the per-order sum of. It carries strictly more structural information (which fee definition, what percentage) than the Orders-level total.
