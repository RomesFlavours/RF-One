# LEGACY 1.0 — Revision 2

## Universal Model of Value Generation, Transfer, and Evolution

### Premise

**Model:** a coherent set of definitions, relationships, and criteria applicable to any business.

**Business:** an organized system that employs knowledge, people, capabilities, resources, and relationships to generate value.

**Application:** the configuration of the model in relation to identity, objectives, recipients, context, and constraints.

**Purpose:** to generate value and increase the capacity to generate it over time.

**Layers:** Core defines the universal elements; Domains specify their specialized applications; individual businesses determine their concrete configuration.

---

### 1. Authenticity

Authenticity is the substantive correspondence between declared identity and the reality of the offering.

The characteristics underlying that identity must be recognizable, verifiable, and representable as knowledge.

**Authenticity concerns consistency in what a business is, not a particular form of product.**

### 2. Accumulated Heritage

Accumulated heritage comprises knowledge, meanings, practices, and relationships that have been built over time and can be transferred.

Tradition is one possible expression of this heritage, not a universal requirement.

Preservation depends on the value and identity represented, not simply on continued repetition.

### 3. Value

Value is the utility generated by applying knowledge in relation to an objective, a recipient, and a context.

Value created, value recognized, and economic results are conceptually distinct.

Profit is a possible outcome of economic value realization, not the definition or exclusive measure of value.

### 4. Adaptation

Adaptation is the modification of the offering or the organization in response to the characteristics of the recipient and the context.

It is consistent with the model when it increases utility without contradicting identity, commitments, or constraints.

Boundaries, responsibilities, and methods of application must be defined by the organization, not left to individual interpretation.

### 5. Evaluation

Evaluation is the comparison of expected value, required resources, risks, and alternatives.

Expected value is a hypothesis; observed value derives from the available evidence.

Indicators, time horizons, and measurement criteria depend on the application. Uncertainty must be explicit.

**A forecast does not constitute proof of the outcome.**

### 6. Reputation

Reputation is the trust accumulated in a business’s ability to remain true to its identity and fulfill its commitments.

It is an asset that can grow, deteriorate, and be restored.

Managing it includes preventing harm, acknowledging consequences, and restoring reliability.

### 7. Innovation

Innovation is the introduction of new possibilities for generating value.

Its evaluation distinguishes the defining elements of identity from the contingent forms of product, process, and organization.

**Preserving value does not require keeping the solutions that produced it unchanged.**

### 8. Organizational Knowledge

Organizational knowledge is the usable body of experience, interpretations, criteria, relationships, and results.

It includes instructions, rationale, conditions of validity, and limitations.

Its evolution connects experience and abstraction:

**Case → understanding → criterion → new application → verification.**

Knowledge sharing must increase both collective capability and the value of individual contributions.

### 9. Recurrence and Reusability

Recurrence signals a possible structural cause and an opportunity for learning.

A solution becomes reusable when it can be applied to an identifiable class of situations.

**Internal need, reusable capability, and commercial opportunity are distinct objects**, each subject to its own evaluation.

### 10. Time and Attention

Time, attention, and decision-making capacity are finite resources.

Freeing them must reduce avoidable commitments and unnecessary dependencies.

Their subsequent allocation is a separate decision: the availability of a resource does not automatically determine its use.

### 11. Continuity and Transferability

Continuity is the ability to generate value independently of the continued presence of individual knowledge holders.

Transferability concerns rationale, criteria, relationships, and learning capabilities, not merely procedures and results.

**What must be transferred is the ability to decide, not merely the record of decisions.**

The inherited body of knowledge guides new choices without replacing the responsibility of those who make them.

### 12. Generativity

Generativity is the ability to transform the value produced and the experience gained into subsequent opportunities for value creation.

New applications may involve the same activity, new offerings, new markets, or new businesses.

**Expansion is a possible outcome, not a requirement of the model.**

### 13. Capability

A capability is the organized ability to produce a class of results through knowledge, people, resources, and processes.

Products and services are particular applications of capabilities.

The value of a capability depends on its usability, transferability, and evolution, not solely on the result already produced.

### 14. Asymmetry

An asymmetry is a difference in the availability, combination, or use of knowledge, resources, and capabilities relative to alternatives.

It can become an advantage when it produces meaningful utility in the relevant context.

Evaluation includes recipients, alternatives, market size, and the evolution of demand.

**A difference is not automatically an advantage.**

### 15. Connection

A connection is a functional relationship between previously separate areas of knowledge, capabilities, or contexts.

Transfer requires distinguishing invariant elements from specific conditions of application.

Its value lies in the possibility of generating new understanding, new capabilities, or better results.

### 17. People

People are autonomous sources of knowledge, judgment, initiative, relationships, and value.

Their contribution depends on the interaction between competencies, motivations, autonomy, responsibility, and organizational conditions.

The system must support their actions and incorporate their contributions, without reducing them to mere task performers or assuming automatic behavior.

### 18. Listening and Feedback

Listening is the systematic acquisition of evidence from the recipients of the offering.

Decision-maker, purchaser, payer, and user are potentially distinct roles.

Feedback assesses utility, the promise, and the experience, and informs organizational knowledge.

**Listening does not mean satisfying every preference; it means testing one’s own assessments against reality.**

### 19. Verification and Learning

Verification compares objectives, hypotheses, and results.

Reassessment may lead to continuation, correction, scaling down, suspension, or termination.

Learning incorporates evidence into the future capacity to understand and decide.

Past commitments do not replace the justification required for future choices.

**The model itself is also subject to verification and evolution.**

---

## Conclusion — Brand

**The Brand is the recognizable manifestation of the value generation model in the specific identity of a business.**

It is defined by the coherent relationship between:

**Purpose → values → identity and positioning → promise → decisions and behaviors → offering and experience.**

Sustaining this consistency makes the Brand recognizable; feedback from experience shapes its reputation. 

**The model defines the universal logic.
The Brand expresses its distinctive configuration.
The Legacy makes its generative capacity transferable and able to evolve.**
