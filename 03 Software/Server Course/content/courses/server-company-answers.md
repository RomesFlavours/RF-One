# Company answers for the Server Course

Rome's Flavours' current answers on allergies, emergencies, mistakes, alcohol, hygiene, and Clover and seats. The first ones are kept from the earlier Server Trainer manual (section 4) where they agree with the master course; alcohol, the allergy ticket, hygiene, and Clover and seats are the owner's answers of 5 November 2026. Roles are named, never people: "the lead on shift" is whoever has that role on the shift. Teach these as facts, with the reason. Where the master course says something different, the master course wins.

## Allergies and dietary limits

- Allergies and religious dietary limits are treated like an injury: prevented with the utmost care.
- A recipe is never changed to remove an allergen. Taking an ingredient off doesn't remove traces of it.
- The Server tells the guest which dishes don't contain it, from the allergen information confirmed by the kitchen. Until the kitchen has confirmed a dish's allergens, the Server checks with the kitchen before answering.
- The Server tells the kitchen about every allergy, every time, even on a dish they know well. Recipes, suppliers and preparation can change.
- If the Server isn't sure, the answer is no, and the guest chooses another dish.
- Children at a table call for extra precautions.
- When a guest reports an allergy, the Server enters an allergy alert on that item in Clover, naming the allergen. The Server also tells the kitchen lead directly and confirms whether the dish can be prepared safely.
- Both restaurants use the same menu, so there is one master allergen chart for every dish. The kitchen lead prepares, verifies and signs it; the General Manager gives final approval.
- The chart is still being written from the menu and the standard recipes. It isn't final until every ingredient and cross-contact risk is verified. Until then, the Server checks every allergy with the kitchen lead, every time.

## Alcohol

- Any guest who appears under 40 shows valid government-issued photo ID before being served alcohol.
- No alcohol is served to anyone under 21.
- A Server may refuse alcohol to a guest who appears intoxicated. The Server tells the FOH manager or the manager on duty, who supports the decision and helps with any issue.
- A new hire doesn't serve alcohol on their own until they've completed the restaurant's training and a manager has approved it. Until then, alcohol is served with a lead.

## Hygiene and illness

- Wash hands with soap and running water for at least 20 seconds: before handling food, after the restroom, after touching anything contaminated, and whenever needed to prevent contamination.
- If you have symptoms that could affect food safety, report them before your shift starts. Front of house reports to the FOH manager; back of house reports to the kitchen lead and the General Manager.
- Florida requires approved food handler training within 60 days of hire, renewed every three years. Rome's Flavours uses SafeStaff. Certificates are kept in the employee's record.

## Clover, seats and payments

- Both locations use Clover. Orders are entered seat by seat: select the right table, enter the number of guests, and put each item on the right seat. Seat numbers stay the same for the whole service.
- Each restaurant has its own numbered table map. The lead shows it to you; the detailed Clover steps and the maps for each location are still being written.
- Split check: ask the guests how they want to divide it, use Clover's check splitting, and check each check before taking payment.
- Declined card: discreetly ask for another payment method. If it can't be solved, tell the FOH manager or the manager on duty.
- Clover down: tell management immediately. Orders may be written by hand when the manager says so. Payments follow the approved backup procedure; usually Clover takes the card as an offline payment and processes it automatically when the system is back. Every transaction is reconciled once the system is back.

## Emergencies and a guest who's been harmed

- Safety is the first goal.
- In an emergency or accident, to you or anyone else, call the manager on shift immediately. If you're alone, call 911.
- If a guest has been harmed or has an allergic reaction: take care of the guest first, write down what they say with the date, time, table and what they ate, don't argue, don't admit anything and don't promise anything. Tell the manager on shift immediately; the manager informs the owner right away. Never reply anywhere public.

## Mistakes and problems

- If you make a mistake, tell the kitchen and the lead on shift right away.
- Asking for help is not evidence of inability. Reporting a mistake or flagging a problem is not, in itself, negative evidence. What makes a mistake bigger is the time before anyone hears about it.
- The common base says it this way: if I can't achieve the result, I make the problem visible early, before it becomes somebody else's problem.
- If you have a problem, including with a colleague, talk to the lead. Don't complain to colleagues.

## Changing an order already sent to the kitchen

- Tell the guest you'll check whether it's already been started, without promising the change.
- Go to the lead on shift right away; they decide with the kitchen.
- Speed matters: the sooner the kitchen knows, the less food and work may be wasted.

## Who decides what

Roles, not names: the leads on each shift are told at the start of the shift.

| Situation | Who |
|---|---|
| The floor, sections, the service | The lead on shift |
| The kitchen, dishes, recipes, allergen confirmation | The kitchen lead on shift |
| The allergen chart | Prepared, verified and signed by the kitchen lead; final approval by the General Manager |
| Refusing alcohol to a guest who appears intoxicated | The Server may refuse; the FOH manager or manager on duty supports the decision |
| A new hire serving alcohol on their own | Only after the restaurant's training and a manager's approval |
| Feeling sick before a shift | Front of house: the FOH manager. Back of house: the kitchen lead and the General Manager |
| A declined card that can't be solved, Clover down | The FOH manager or the manager on duty |
| A change to an order already sent | The lead on shift, with the kitchen |
| Any gesture or compensation for a guest | Only the person with that authority, through the lead on shift. The Server never grants it. Amounts and levels: to be confirmed, never stated. |
| An injury, a guest harmed, an allergic reaction, a food safety problem, an alcohol problem | The manager on shift, immediately. If you're alone in an emergency, 911. |

Every gesture for a guest is recorded. Recording it and approving it are two separate things.
