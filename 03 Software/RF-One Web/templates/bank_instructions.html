The monthly work

Bank reconciliation is done once a month, for the previous month, on Import and Review. The page opens on the previous calendar month (local time of the Location); any other month can be chosen there.

  1. Import — upload the month's original bank and card downloads.
  2. Check Sources — confirm that every account and card that should have produced a download for the month did, and explain the ones that did not.
  3. Automatic WHO — see how many of the month's transactions already have their counterparty (WHO), and where it came from.
  4. Review Missing — complete, in Review, the transactions RF-One could not resolve automatically.

What source files to load

The source of truth is the original monthly download from each issuer, uploaded as it was downloaded — bank accounts and cards (Chase, First Citizens, American Express). No spreadsheet is ever consulted.

Leave the Payment Instrument on automatic unless RF-One asks. A file that carries its own identifier (First Citizens Account Number, Chase Card) always resolves by that identifier; a file name is only ever a hint, never authoritative.

A file whose exact bytes were already imported is recognised and not imported again, whatever its name. The month a file belongs to comes from the dates inside it, never from its name or from the day it was uploaded.

Source completeness

One question only: did every bank and card that should have produced an original download this month actually produce one? This is not the accounting close, not reconciliation completion and not P&L approval — a month can be source-complete while every transaction in it is still unclassified.

A month can be marked COMPLETE only when every expected account or card is covered by a source file or explicitly resolved by a person. There is no override.

A missing source file never closes or deactivates an account or card. Only the explicit Closed / Lost / Replaced / Other decisions do that, and only because an operator chose one by name; they are dated from the instrument's last posting date, which RF-One derives itself. A closed instrument is kept permanently for historical audit and matching — it is never deleted, and its last four and identity are never rewritten.

Overlapping date ranges are allowed — a corrected or reissued file is legitimate. No transaction is counted twice: that is enforced by the canonical transaction deduplication, not by rejecting the file.

Controlled, historical, provisional

  • Controlled — the month is on or after the reconciliation control start. Controlled months are opened automatically when an import covers them, and follow the ordinary completeness rules.
  • Historical — the month is before the control start. Its transactions are imported and kept, but RF-One does not open it, does not demand its missing accounts, and never certifies it complete just because data exists.
  • Provisional — the month is after the validated-through month. Its data is kept and shown, but it is not yet certified complete, so its gaps are shown and not enforced, and it cannot be completed until the validated-through month reaches it.
  • Not under completeness control — no control start is configured, so no month is placed under control automatically. Imported data is still kept normally.

Setting the control start for the first time, or moving it earlier, also opens the controlled months covered by files already imported — no file has to be uploaded again. Moving it later changes nothing that already exists. Both settings are on Monthly Sources.

WHO means identity, not purpose

The WHO is the counterparty of a transaction — who was paid, or who paid. Recognising who was paid says nothing about why: a WHO never determines the WHY or the WHAT of a transaction by itself.

When a person confirms a WHO in Review, RF-One can learn that the same bank description means the same WHO. On the next import that learned rule supplies the WHO automatically. It recognises the counterparty only, and leaves the purpose of each transaction to that transaction's own evidence, or to a person.

Automatic WHO on Import and Review shows, for the month, how many transactions have a WHO from a learned rule, from a human decision, or none yet. Bulk authorization of automatic WHO completion is not available: it is pending a functional decision.

Manual review

Transactions RF-One could not resolve automatically are completed in Review Transactions, one by one. Import and Review counts them for the month and opens Review on that month. A transaction counts as still needing review until it has a resolved decision, or is a confirmed internal transfer — the same definition the Monthly Export uses to block an export.

Saved source rules

A source whose own content cannot identify its account (First Citizens' AccountHistory.csv, whose name never changes) is resolved once by a human; that choice is saved and reused on the next import. A rule is always overridden by a reliable identifier inside the file, and disabling one never deletes it. Saved source rules are on Instruments.

Accounting deduplication

The same operation can reach the books twice — from a card and its linked card, from two Chase downloads, twice in one file, or from files saved under different names. Rows that share a settlement account, posting date, signed amount and normalized payee are one accounting fact: one canonical transaction feeds accounting, the others are kept, linked and excluded. No raw row is ever deleted.

A transaction whose card has no settlement account configured for its posting date is not deduplicated and not merged with anything: configure the card's settlement account on Instruments, then recompute.
