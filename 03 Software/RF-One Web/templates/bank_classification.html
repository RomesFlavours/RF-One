Classification

A bank movement is classified as Who → Why → What. What is the final accounting classification — a Profit & Loss or a Balance Sheet destination. Every Why resolves to one destination, and every Who resolves to one default Why and inherits its What. That is why the Review asks only for the Who.

Changing an association here applies to future classifications. Transactions already confirmed keep the classification they were decided with; applying a changed chain to one of them is the explicit Reclassify action on the Review page, which records a new auditable decision instead of rewriting the old one.

{# ------------------------------------------------ Invoice boundary ------ #}

Suppliers paid by invoice

A bank movement says who was paid, not what was bought. A supplier paid against an invoice is classified here as the settlement it is — typically an Accounts Payable settlement on the Balance Sheet. The Food Cost / Operating / Deductible composition comes from the invoice (Invoice Intake / Purchased), never from the bank line: a single Costco, Instacart, Sam's, Cheney Brothers or Prime Line payment routinely covers several cost families at once, and splitting it from the bank side would be invention.

Where the invoice or its link is missing, that is reported as a matching gap. Building the Bank ↔ Invoice matching itself is deliberately out of scope here — this states the boundary, nothing more.
